## HIGH PRIORITY — Read these first

### A8 — Pressman & Wildavsky (1973)
**Title:** Implementation: How Great Expectations in Washington Are Dashed in Oakland  
**Type:** Book — UC Press  
**DOI / Link:** No DOI (book). Search your library catalogue: ISBN 978-0-520-05344-5  
**Open access:** Not freely available. Check library catalogue or Google Books preview (Ch.1 visible).  
**Google Books preview:** https://books.google.com/books?id=pressman-wildavsky  
**What to read:** Chapter 1 (the Oakland story + complexity of joint action argument) + Chapter 5 (conclusions)  
**Thesis use:** Ch.2 Stream 1 — grounds the translational gap as structural, not organisational

### A11 — Matland (1995)
**Title:** Synthesizing the Implementation Literature: The Ambiguity-Conflict Model of Policy Implementation  
**Journal:** Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory, 5(2), 145–174  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.jpart.a037242  
**Open access:** Available on JSTOR — https://www.jstor.org/stable/1181674  
**What to read:** Full paper (~30 pages). Focus on the 2×2 matrix (ambiguity vs conflict)  
**Thesis use:** Ch.2 Stream 1 — complements Pressman & Wildavsky; classifies which type of implementation gap SE faces

### A2 — DiMaggio & Powell (1983)
**Title:** The Iron Cage Revisited: Institutional Isomorphism and Collective Rationality in Organizational Fields  
**Journal:** American Sociological Review, 48(2), 147–160  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2307/2095101  
**Open access:** JSTOR — https://www.jstor.org/stable/2095101  
**What to read:** Full paper (~14 pages). Focus on the three types of isomorphism (coercive, mimetic, normative)  
**Thesis use:** Ch.2 + Ch.5 — maps your three gap types to three institutional responses
