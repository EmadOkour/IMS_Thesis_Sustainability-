## Paragraph 3 — Research Gap

SE operates as a multi-tier web connecting multiple businesses with multiple activities across different countries, carrying legal responsibility for human rights and environmental impacts along its entire value chain. Its governance mechanism does not however extend to Tier 3 suppliers, highlighting the gap between the legal coverage required under LkSG and the actual reach of SE's self-assessments and external audits. Furthermore, while the EU Taxonomy classifies activities such as wind turbine manufacturing as fully aligned, it does not extend its criteria to the upstream extraction of the minerals required to manufacture those turbines — a boundary condition with significant environmental implications. A systematic analytical approach to SE's FY2025 Sustainability Statement, examining why activities fail to achieve aligned status, reveals that gaps cluster into three categories based on the type of intervention required to close them: a process fix, a transformation fix, or a regulatory fix.

*Sources: LkSG s.2; EU 2021/2139 Annex I A3.1 DNSH; SE Annual Report 2025 s.2.10.2.3*

## Paragraph 4 — Gap Classification

A single intervention cannot address these gaps due to their non-uniform nature. Where gaps arise from missing documentation or incomplete data collection, process improvement within management systems could close the gap. However, process improvement alone cannot achieve alignment for activities that are structurally non-compliant with the technical screening criteria of the EU Taxonomy. Similarly, certain environmentally superior activities remain outside alignment not because of organisational failure, but rather because the relevant technical standards are not referenced in the EU Delegated Regulation 2021/2139. A governance framework capable of addressing all three gap types systematically and of integrating taxonomy alignment requirements across business area boundaries at the operational level is the central contribution of this thesis.

*Sources: EU Delegated Regulation 2021/2139 Annex I A3.20 (EN standards reference); SE Annual Report 2025 s.2.10.2.3 p.86*

## Paragraph 5 — Proposed Framework

SE's Zero Harm Framework shows that complex regulatory obligations can be cascaded systematically through a three-layer architecture of principles, behaviours, and Essentials. This thesis proposes a Taxonomy Compliance Framework that mirrors this proven architecture for EU Taxonomy and CSRD obligations, translating regulatory requirements into three layers: a Policy layer anchored in CSRD disclosure obligations, a Building Blocks layer comprising specific auditable modules for each taxonomy-relevant activity, and an Essentials layer defining mandatory requirements for high-taxonomy-risk supplier interactions. Embedded within the Plan, Do, Check and Act logic of ISO management systems, the framework allows the transformation of taxonomy compliance from a static annual reporting exercise to a continuously improving governance process.

*Sources: SE Annual Report 2025, s.2.10.2.2.2, p.79 (Zero Harm aligned with ISO 14001 through building blocks); ISO 9001:2015 clause 0.3.2 (PDCA cycle)*

## Paragraph 6 — Research Question

ESRS E1-1 requires companies to disclose their transition plan and strategies on decarbonization and prove their climate change resilience. SE does not disclose that in their report and, along with the gap findings, reflects the need for a systematic governance framework that covers the full reach of their legal requirement under LkSG, CSRD and EU Taxonomy. How can sustainability obligations arising from the EU Taxonomy and CSRD be systematically translated from organisational policy level to operational implementation across the full hierarchy of CSRD-obligated industrial manufacturing companies, and what governance framework is required to close that translational gap? This question is examined through three supporting inquiries: the structural gaps between ISO-based processes and taxonomy criteria, the governance architecture required for cross-BA boundary management, and the design of a cascading compliance framework modelled on SE's Zero Harm architecture.

*Sources: ESRS E1-1; EU Delegated Regulation 2021/2139; SE Annual Report 2025, s.2.10.2.1.6, p.72*
